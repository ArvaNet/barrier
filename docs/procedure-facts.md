# Barrier: procedures fact-check

Checked 2026-10-21. 7 of 8 web searches used, plus direct fetches of the primary pages. Reddit not used.

Rule for all Procedures copy: the app records what the user's clinic told them and never sets timing itself. Where a clinic's instructions differ from anything below, the clinic wins. Say that once, near anything about pausing or timing, and use hedges ("many clinics", "often", "may") instead of rules. Never show a number of days or weeks as a rule.

Method: "VERIFIED" means I read the sentence on the page itself via a direct fetch (aad.org, my.clevelandclinic.org, dermnetnz.org, NHS trust leaflets, nhs.uk, a BAD patient leaflet PDF). "Search only" means it appeared in a search summary and I did not read it on the page. Not found: an AAD page specifically on laser resurfacing aftercare (the AAD pages I could reach cover laser for scars, microneedling, chemical peels, and acne scar care). nhs.uk has no peel, laser or microneedling page that I found; NHS evidence below comes from hospital-trust leaflets and the nhs.uk isotretinoin page. A British Association of Dermatologists leaflet was reached only for isotretinoin.

## 1. Retinoids paused before/after peels, laser, microneedling

VERDICT: PARTLY confirmed. Several reputable sources do say clinics ask for a pause, but the length varies from "a few days" to 4 weeks, differs by procedure, and one source says the opposite (start a retinoid before laser). Only usable as "some clinics ask".

What the sources say (all VERIFIED on the page unless marked):
- AAD, microneedling: a 2-to-4-week prep routine "may include stopping use of retinoids (i.e., retinol and tretinoin)" or other ingredients "for a few days". Nothing about restarting afterwards. https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/scars-stretch-marks/microneedling-fade-scars
- Cleveland Clinic, microneedling: stop exfoliants "such as salicylic acid and Retin-A" about one week before. https://my.clevelandclinic.org/cosmetic-plastic-surgery/procedures/microneedling (no page date)
- Cleveland Clinic, chemical peels (reviewed 03/22/2021): "Don't use any products containing retinoids (such as tretinoin) one to two weeks before treatment", with an exception if your physician says otherwise. https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11010-chemical-peels
- AAD, laser treatment for scars: stop skin care products with a retinoid or glycolic acid "for 2 to 4 weeks" before. (No date on page.) https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/scars-stretch-marks/laser-treatment-scar
- NHS hospital-trust leaflet (Queen Victoria Hospital, CO2 laser for scars, approved Feb 2025): prescribed topical retinoids (tretinoin, adapalene) stopped 2 weeks before laser "because it may lead to delayed healing and scarring". https://www.qvh.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Carbon-Dioxide-C02-Laser-0726-WEB-VERSION.pdf
- Cleveland Clinic, laser resurfacing (reviewed 03/22/2021): after laser, "avoid aggressive facial treatments, such as tretinoin or glycolic acid for four weeks" and irritating topicals for six weeks. This is the only after-procedure retinoid timing I found. The same page says you "may be asked to apply a topical retinoid ... for about four weeks before your procedure" (the opposite of pausing). https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11015-laser-skin-resurfacing
- AAD acne scar treatment page (updated 12/8/23) also lists retinoids as possible pre-peel priming. https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/acne/derm-treat/scars/treatment

Where it is silent: AAD chemical peel FAQs mention only a "pre-peel skin care plan for 2 to 4 weeks" without naming products. The NHS Chelsea and Westminster microneedling pre-treatment leaflet has no retinoid advice at all (sun and screening only). AAD acne scar aftercare says only "If your dermatologist recommended skin care products, use them." I found no reputable page giving a restart date after a peel or microneedling. A search summary claimed "many dermatologists restart once skin has healed"; I could not source it. Leave restart timing out.

Typical ranges, only as "often" and only for background (do not show in the UI): a few days to 2 weeks before microneedling and peels, 2 to 4 weeks before laser, up to about 4 weeks after laser (one source).

App-ready: "Some clinics ask you to pause retinoids for a while before or after a procedure, and others don't. Only pause if your clinic tells you to, and follow their dates."

## 1b. Extra safety item found: oral isotretinoin

VERDICT: CONFIRMED (VERIFIED), worth a prompt in the Procedures form.
- nhs.uk (reviewed 26 June 2026): while taking isotretinoin, do not have "skin treatments such as microdermabrasion or chemical peels", or waxing or laser hair removal. https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/isotretinoin-capsules/side-effects-of-isotretinoin-capsules/ (no wait time stated)
- BAD patient leaflet (isotretinoin; July 2019, review date July 2022 is past): while taking it "and for six months afterwards", avoid waxing, epilation, dermabrasion, laser. https://cdn.bad.org.uk/uploads/2021/12/29200301/Isotretinoin-Updated-July-2019-lay-reviewed-June-2019-amended-January-2020.pdf
- Cleveland Clinic microneedling: stop Accutane six months before. QVH leaflet: oral retinoids 6 months before laser.
- A search summary said BAD gives "one year"; the leaflet I read says six months. Not used.

App-ready: "If you take or recently took isotretinoin tablets, tell your clinic before any procedure."

## 2. Sun protection after laser and peels

VERDICT: CONFIRMED.
- AAD, laser for scars: "Sun protection is crucial before and after laser treatment"; sun reaching treated skin can cause another scar; you cannot be treated with a tan or sunburn. https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/scars-stretch-marks/laser-treatment-scar
- AAD, acne scar aftercare (updated 12/8/23): apply sunscreen every day to treated skin, broad-spectrum, water-resistant, SPF 30 or higher, and avoid too much time outdoors for 6 to 8 weeks. This page does not name a specific procedure. https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/acne/derm-treat/scars/self-care
- AAD, chemical peels FAQs: "After your skin heals, apply sunscreen every day"; total sun avoidance until healed after medium peels. https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/younger-looking/chemical-peels-faqs
- Cleveland Clinic, laser resurfacing: "avoid the sun as much as possible and apply sunscreen every day"; broad-spectrum after healing. https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11015-laser-skin-resurfacing
- DermNet, skin needling (June 2022): "Sun protection for several weeks is recommended." https://dermnetnz.org/topics/skin-needling
- Durations differ by source (AAD 6 to 8 weeks, DermNet "several weeks", NHS trust leaflet 6 months for CO2 laser on scars). Do not state a duration.

App-ready: "Sun protection matters before and after procedures. Many clinics advise avoiding tanning and wearing a broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher every day on treated skin; follow your clinic's instructions on how long."

## 3. Acne scar treatments: gradual results, often several sessions

VERDICT: CONFIRMED for microneedling, laser and peels. Subcision: PARTLY (DermNet only; AAD's acne scar pages do not name subcision).
- AAD, microneedling: "Results appear gradually as your body produces collagen"; improvement may show "within a few weeks"; it "usually takes several months before you see full results"; for acne scars "you may need 3 to 5 treatments, which are performed every 2 to 4 weeks".
- AAD, laser for scars: "It can take months to see the results ... and you may notice little improvement at first"; "You may need more than one laser treatment".
- AAD, acne scar treatment (12/8/23): "your dermatologist may use more than one treatment"; "Some lasers require more treatment sessions than others"; mild peels "3 to 5 treatments". Subcision is not named (closest wording: a dermatologist "surgically lifts the scar from the underlying tissue"). https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/acne/derm-treat/scars/treatment
- DermNet, subcision (Nov 2015): "Three to six visits suffice for the majority of cases of moderate scarring", at least a month apart; can be combined with microneedling, acid peels, TCA CROSS, fractional laser, topical retinoids. No time to results, no bruising or healing duration given. https://dermnetnz.org/topics/subcision
- DermNet, skin needling: results "as soon as 2 weeks and as long as 6-8 months"; moderate acne scars "three to four treatments may be needed". Cleveland Clinic microneedling: "three to six months for best results".
- Intervals disagree (AAD 2 to 4 weeks, DermNet at least 6 weeks, NHS Chelsea and Westminster burns leaflet at least 6 weeks), so do not show an interval.
- A search summary also cited ClinicalTrials.gov protocol pages for subcision; those are trial registrations, not guidance, so not used.

App-ready: "Scar treatments usually work gradually over weeks to months, and many people need more than one session. Your clinic will tell you how many and how far apart."

## 4. Photos before and after

VERDICT: PARTLY confirmed.
- AAD, how to take pictures of your skin for your dermatologist (updated 7/20/23): photos help a dermatologist "see how a rash, growth on your skin, or other concern is changing" and "determine how well your treatment is working". Tips on the page: natural bright light (a window), light on the area and not behind you, neutral one-colour background, a close-up that shows detail, a shot with surrounding skin for comparison, remove makeup. https://www.aad.org/public/fad/digital-health/taking-pictures-skin
- NHS trust leaflet (QVH): clinics photograph the treatment area before treatment, with consent, "so that the progress of your treatment can be monitored", and patients are asked to note how long the area takes to heal, which "may be used to inform future treatments".
- NOT on the AAD page: flash advice, same distance/angle each time, a rhythm like "every 4 to 6 weeks", and "wait until swelling settles". Those are common sense, not dermatology-sourced. Fine as app tips, not as cited facts.
- The AAD page is about photos for a dermatologist, not before-and-after photos specifically for scar procedures.

App-ready: "Photos in the same natural light can help you and your dermatologist see how a treatment is working over time. If you can, take one before your procedure."

## 5. What the app should NOT say

1. Any number of days or weeks to pause retinoids as a rule. Sources range from "a few days" to 4 weeks, differ by procedure, and one (Cleveland Clinic laser page) says some clinics ask you to start a retinoid before laser. Let the user type their clinic's dates.
2. Any restart date or "safe to resume" message. No reputable page I reached gives a restart date after peels or microneedling. The app should only show the date the user entered.
3. Healing times or downtime as fact. Examples of how much they vary: microneedling redness "up to five days" (Cleveland Clinic), "a few days to a week" (AAD), "1 to 2 days" (an NHS burns-unit leaflet); peels 1 to 7, 7 to 14, 14 to 21 days by depth; laser one to two weeks plus redness for two to three months (Cleveland Clinic). Depth, device, skin type and clinic all change this.
4. "Minimal downtime" or "no downtime" claims (AAD says this for microneedling and for non-ablative scar lasers only).
5. Session counts or intervals as a plan (3 to 5, 3 to 6, "every 2 to 4 weeks" all appear; they are ranges for moderate scars in specific sources).
6. A sunscreen or sun-avoidance duration as a rule (6 to 8 weeks, "several weeks", 6 months all appear).
7. Result promises. AAD: "Treatment is not for everyone" and scars "rarely go away completely on their own"; an NHS trust leaflet says "only limited effects" are occasionally achieved.
8. Any subcision aftercare or downtime. DermNet gives complications but no bruising, swelling or recovery durations, and I found no reputable source that does.
9. Anything that sounds like diagnosing a reaction or infection. If a line is wanted: "Contact your clinic if something looks or feels wrong." (The QVH leaflet lists leaking fluid or pus, excessive redness, heat and swelling as signs to contact the clinic; do not copy that as a diagnostic checklist.)
10. "Cleveland Clinic says" without the date: its peel and laser pages were last reviewed 03/22/2021; DermNet subcision is dated Nov 2015; the BAD isotretinoin leaflet's review date (July 2022) has passed.
11. "AAD says retinoids should be stopped for X" for laser resurfacing. The only AAD retinoid-pause line I read (2 to 4 weeks) is on the scar laser page, and the microneedling page says "a few days".
